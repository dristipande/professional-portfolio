Artifact 1

A Timeline of Artificial Intelligence: From the 1940s to Generative AI

Introduction. This artifact is a short slide deck that traces the history of artificial intelligence, including the two AI winters and the rise of deep learning and generative AI. It is written for professionals who work with data and need to understand where AI came from before deciding how to use it.

Slide 1 of 11

Download the deck: PDF · PowerPoint (.pptx)

Description

The deck has 11 slides. It covers the early foundations (1943–1958), the first and second AI winters, the milestone wins by Deep Blue, Watson, and AlphaGo, the four factors behind deep learning (GPUs, cloud computing, data, and algorithms), the generative AI era, and the leading AI companies today.

Each slide uses short descriptions and visuals instead of long text. Sources were an overview of AI’s evolution (IEEE Computer Society, n.d.), a data-driven history of AI (Bender & Our World in Data, n.d.), and a documentary on AI history (Putchuon - “Put You On,” 2024).

Objective

I created this timeline to understand how AI developed, why progress stalled twice, and what finally made modern AI practical, and to present that history clearly to a non-expert audience.

Process
  • Reviewed the three assigned sources and noted major milestones from the 1950s onward.
  • Identified the two AI winters and wrote down their causes and effects.
  • Grouped events into eras and checked dates and names against the sources.
  • Designed the slides so each one makes a single point, then revised for readability.
Tools and technologies used

PowerPoint-format slide deck; Claude, an AI assistant, for organizing research and drafting, with content reviewed against the assigned sources.

Value proposition

This artifact shows that I can research a complex technical topic, verify it against credible sources, and explain it clearly to people who are not specialists. These are the skills a team needs when deciding how to introduce AI into its work.

Unique value

My perspective as a data engineer shaped the timeline. I put extra emphasis on the data, hardware, and cloud systems behind each breakthrough, since those are often overlooked when AI history is told as a story about algorithms alone.

Relevance

Leaders who understand why AI went through boom-and-bust cycles are better prepared to judge new tools, set realistic goals, and avoid hype. This timeline gives employers and collaborators a quick, accurate overview they can use as a reference.
